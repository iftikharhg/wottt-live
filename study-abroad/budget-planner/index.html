What the embassy asks to see

Most consulates want evidence of one year’s living costs — a fixed amount they set — plus proof that tuition is paid or payable. Some require it in a blocked account; others accept bank statements and a sponsor letter.

The amount is set by each embassy and changes; your consultant gives you the current figure for your destination.

Paying for it

Where the money usually comes from.

Family sponsorship

The most common route. Statements must show steady balances — sudden large deposits are the first thing a visa officer questions.

Scholarships

Government, university and regional awards — never guaranteed, but worth planning around.

Scholarships by destination →

Education loans

If you need an education loan, compare the written terms your bank offers — eligibility, security, repayment and total cost. Ask early: approval takes weeks and the embassy wants the sanction letter, not a promise.

Questions

About the numbers.

Where do I get the figures?

Tuition from the university’s offer or fee page; living costs from the destination guide and your consultant; the visa fund from the embassy’s current rule. We fill the sheet with you in consultation.

Which exchange rate should I use?

Use today’s bank selling rate, then add a margin — ten percent is sensible — because the rupee may move between application and departure.

Is the “funds to show” the same as what I will spend?

No. The embassy figure is a legal minimum you must evidence; actual costs can be lower or higher. Plan for the higher of the two.

Does this page send my figures anywhere?

No. Everything stays in your browser. Print it or screenshot it to share with family or your consultant.
